About Us & Methodology

The 5-Axis Philosophical Compass is a multi-dimensional analytical tool engineered to evaluate and map an individual's worldview across five core philosophical and political dimensions. Conventional political spectrums often reduce complex worldviews to a single Left-Right paradigm or a simple two-axis grid, collapsing distinct ethical, ontological, and social stances into ambiguous categories.

This test isolates five distinct intellectual axes to provide a high-resolution vector representation of your foundational beliefs and social philosophy.

The Five Philosophical Axes Explained

1. Ethics (ETH): Objectivism vs. Subjectivism

This axis measures your stance on moral ontology. Objectivism postulates that moral truths exist independently of human perception, culture, or consensus, holding that good and evil are objective features of reality accessible through reason or divine law. Conversely, Subjectivism views morality as a social construct, evolving cultural agreement, or emotional expression that carries no permanent or universal validity.

2. Human Nature (HN): Realism vs. Constructivism

This dimension evaluates your worldview regarding human potential and social engineering. Realism asserts that human beings possess fixed biological, psychological, and moral traits that fundamentally constrain what is achievable in politics and social organization. Constructivism, on the other hand, holds that human nature is almost infinitely malleable and shaped by environment, education, and institutional arrangements.

3. Tribalism (TRB): Tribalism vs. Universalism

This axis examines the boundary of your moral obligations and community duty. Tribalism emphasizes distinct social identity, group exclusivity, and primary responsibility toward one's own immediate community or nation. Universalism advocates for global interconnectedness, cosmopolitan responsibility, and equal moral consideration for all human beings regardless of citizenship or group identity.

4. Justice (JST): Justice vs. Equality

This scale measures your preference regarding social hierarchy and economic distribution. Justice favors meritocracy, proportional reward based on effort/contribution, and acceptance of natural inequality as a stabilizing force. Equality prioritizes egalitarianism, social safety nets, systemic redistribution, and the reduction of socioeconomic disparities.

5. Religion & Transcendence (RLG): Spiritualism vs. Laicity

This axis assesses the role of metaphysical frameworks in your understanding of existence. Spiritualism acknowledges innate purpose, non-material forces, and the value of faith or prayer in human history. Laicity maintains a strictly materialist framework, viewing empirical science as the sole source of valid knowledge and regarding religious structures as historical artifacts.
